# Taksha Brand Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Brand:** Taksha
**Sector:** Digital Craft Studio

---

## 1. Brand Fundamentals

### 1.1 Brand Story
Taksha comes from the Sanskrit root word **"Takṣ"**, meaning *to carve, shape, craft, build, or create with precision*. We are not a traditional web development agency; we are a premium Digital Craft Studio. We believe that true digital excellence requires a blend of rigorous engineering, thoughtful UI/UX design, and strategic brand positioning. Everything we build is an artifact of craftsmanship.

### 1.2 Mission
To transform visionary ideas into meaningful, high-performance digital experiences through an uncompromising dedication to design, engineering, and artificial intelligence.

### 1.3 Vision
To become the definitive standard for digital craftsmanship—a studio where global innovators turn when they refuse to settle for the ordinary.

### 1.4 Purpose
To eliminate the friction between a great idea and a flawless execution. We exist to build products that look beautiful, feel tangible, and perform flawlessly.

### 1.5 Brand Personality
- **Sophisticated yet Accessible:** We are premium, but not elitist.
- **Precise & Engineered:** We care about the unseen details.
- **Authentic & Transparent:** We do not fabricate case studies, testimonials, or results. We let the work speak for itself.
- **Innovative & Forward-Thinking:** We embrace AI automation and modern technology to amplify human creativity.

### 1.6 Tone of Voice
- **Confident, not Arrogant.** We know our craft, but we remain humble students of technology.
- **Concise & Direct.** We do not use fluff, buzzwords, or corporate jargon. We speak plainly about complex things.
- **Inspiring.** We elevate the conversation to focus on the art of the possible.

## 2. Visual Identity

### 2.1 Logo Usage
The Taksha logo is our most critical visual asset. It consists of the "T" Mark and the Wordmark.
- **Primary Logo:** Full lockup (Mark + Wordmark + "Crafting Digital Excellence" tagline where appropriate).
- **Secondary Logo:** The isolated "T" Mark. Used in tight spaces, avatars, and UI navigation (if the full wordmark is too wide).

### 2.2 Logo Safe Area
The minimum safe area around the logo is defined by the height of the "T" in the wordmark (referred to as *1x*). No other graphic elements, text, or page edges should encroach into this space.

### 2.3 Logo Variations
- **Navy/Dark BG:** Use the White/Amber logo variation.
- **White/Light BG:** Use the Navy/Amber logo variation.
- **Monochrome:** Solid Navy (on light) or Solid White (on dark) for printing constraints.

### 2.4 Incorrect Logo Usage
- **Do not** stretch, skew, or distort the logo.
- **Do not** change the logo colors outside of the approved palette.
- **Do not** add drop shadows or glow effects to the primary logo.
- **Do not** place the logo on a busy background or photograph where contrast is lost.
- **Do not** alter the proportion between the mark and the wordmark.

## 3. Color & Typography

### 3.1 Color Palette
Our color palette reflects a bold, Neo-Brutalist design language emphasizing high contrast and sharp edges.
- **Cream / Off-White:** `#FAF9F6` (Primary background, space)
- **Pure Black:** `#000000` (Thick borders, heavy text, solid drop shadows)
- **Bright Yellow:** `#FFC107` (Primary accent, energy, call-to-action)
- **Pastel Accents:** Mint (`#A7E4D0`), Lilac (`#C4B5FD`), Blue (`#93C5FD`) (Used for solid card backgrounds)

### 3.2 Typography Rules
- **Primary Typeface (Headings):** `Outfit` — Geometric, modern, and highly legible. Use for Hero headers (H1, H2) and primary branding.
- **Secondary Typeface (Body):** `Inter` — Built for screens. Use for all body copy, paragraphs, and UI data.
- **Monospace:** `JetBrains Mono` — For any technical representations or code snippets.
- **Hierarchy:** Always establish a clear visual hierarchy. Use contrasting weights (e.g., Bold for headers, Regular for body) rather than just size differences.

## 4. Visual Elements

### 4.1 Iconography
- Custom-drawn, stroke-based SVGs.
- Stroke width: Consistent `1.5px` to `2px`.
- Style: Minimalist, geometric, no unnecessary flourishes.

### 4.2 Photography Style
- **Authentic & Real:** No stock-photo clichés. Show real devices, actual code environments, or abstract macro-shots of textures (glass, metal, dark matter).
- **Lighting:** Moody, dramatic lighting with deep shadows. Often utilizing Navy or Amber light tints to integrate with the brand.
- **Subject:** Focused, tightly cropped, high resolution.

### 4.3 Illustration Style
- Avoid cartoonish or generic corporate illustrations (e.g., flat "tech bro" vectors).
- Use 3D renders, glassmorphic abstract shapes, or isometric grid-based technical drawings that emphasize the concept of "building" and "crafting."

### 4.4 Graphic Elements
- **Solid Shadows & Thick Borders:** Elements use stark black outlines (e.g., `3px solid #000`) and unblurred black drop shadows offset at `4px` or `8px`.
- **Flat Colors:** No gradients, no glassmorphism. Colors are solid and unapologetic.
- **Geometric Grid:** Faint polka-dot grid backgrounds to reinforce a technical yet raw canvas feel.
- **Corner Folds (Dog-Ears):** Cards feature a stark black triangle cutout in the bottom right corner.

### 4.5 Motion Principles
- **Physics-Based:** Animations should feel natural, adhering to mass and spring physics.
- **Purposeful:** Do not animate for the sake of animation. Motion should guide the user's eye to important actions.
- **Snappy:** Keep UI interactions (hovers, clicks) under `150ms`.

## 5. Application

### 5.1 Grid Usage (Print & Web)
- Rely on a strict 12-column grid for digital applications.
- For print (proposals, letterheads), adhere to classic Swiss grid principles with generous whitespace (negative space).

### 5.2 Social Media Style
- **Visuals:** High-contrast Navy backgrounds with bold Amber typography.
- **Content:** Educational, behind-the-scenes engineering, UI/UX breakdowns.
- **Format:** Carousel posts showing the "before & after" of digital transformations.

### 5.3 Presentation Style (Decks)
- Deep dark backgrounds.
- 1 core idea per slide.
- Massive typography for key metrics or statements.
- Minimal text; rely on verbal delivery and striking imagery.

## 6. Stationery & Collateral

### 6.1 Business Card
- **Material:** Ultra-thick, matte dark navy cardstock.
- **Finish:** Amber foil stamping for the logo mark.
- **Content:** Minimalist. Name, Title, `taksha.com`, Email. No physical address unless legally required.

### 6.2 Letterhead
- Clean, generous margins. Logo top left. Contact details bottom right in small, tracked-out caps.

### 6.3 Email Signature
- Simple text.
- **Format:** `[Name] | [Title] | Taksha | taksha.com`
- Avoid embedding heavy images or secondary logos.

### 6.4 Proposal Design
- Structured like a high-end editorial magazine.
- Include a personalized cover, executive summary, technical architecture proposal, and transparent pricing.
- Bound beautifully if printed, or presented via a custom web-link.

### 6.5 Portfolio Style
- **Focus on the Work:** UI mockups should be presented edge-to-edge or inside realistic device frames.
- **Explain the "Why":** Every portfolio piece must include the problem, the technical/design solution, and the craftsmanship involved.

## 7. Digital & UI

### 7.1 UI Style Guide
- **Buttons:** Sharp edges (`0px` radius). Thick black borders, solid yellow backgrounds, mechanical press active states (translates down to cover shadow).
- **Forms:** Clean, thick-bordered inputs with solid focus states.
- **Light Mode Default:** The brand is built around high-contrast Neo-Brutalist light mode; no dark mode is currently supported.
- **For full digital UI specifications, refer to the [Taksha Design System PRD].**

## 8. Communication

### 8.1 Writing Guidelines
- **Be ruthless with editing.** If a sentence can be cut, cut it.
- **Active Voice:** Write "We engineered a solution" instead of "A solution was engineered by us."
- **Capitalization:** Use Sentence case for headings (e.g., "Crafting digital excellence") to feel more modern, except for formal product names.

### 8.2 Messaging Framework
- **Value Proposition:** "We build what others can only imagine."
- **Elevator Pitch:** "Taksha is a premium digital craft studio that merges world-class UI/UX design with rigorous frontend engineering and AI automation to deliver exceptional digital experiences."
- **Key Pillars:**
  1. Uncompromising Craft
  2. Technical Excellence
  3. Total Transparency
